# Summary

The Indian Philosophy curriculum is a **complete education in Indian Philosophy** using online materials.

## Organization

This repository is organized into three main components:

- **Core Curriculum** (this page): the foundational knowledge of the field;
- **[Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md)**: focused study in specific areas;
- **[Projects](projects.md)**: support learning through practical application throughout the curriculum.

**Process:** Learners may work through the curriculum independently or collaboratively, and either sequentially or selectively.

- For simplicity, courses in the Core Curriculum are ordered according to their prerequisites.
- The Core Curriculum provides a shared foundation and is intended to be completed in full.
- Advanced Topics are optional; learners are encouraged to select one area of focus and complete all courses within that topic.

Practical work is integrated through the [Projects section](projects.md) and may be undertaken alongside coursework.

Note: When there are courses or books that don't fit into the curriculum but are otherwise of high quality,
they belong in [extras/courses](extras/courses.md), [extras/readings](extras/readings.md).

## How to use this curriculum

### Core Sections

These sections provide the structured, comprehensive foundation necessary to understand classical and medieval Indian philosophy. Study them **in this exact order**:

1. **Foundations: Orientation and Historical Context**
   Start here to master foundational vocabulary (*darśana*, *pramāṇa*, *śāstra*), the Vedic and Upaniṣadic roots, and the classification of Indian philosophical systems into orthodox (*āstika*) and heterodox (*nāstika*) traditions.

2. **Heterodox Traditions (Nāstika Systems)**
   Next, examine the non-Vedic schools: the radical empiricism and materialism of Cārvāka, the psychological and metaphysical insights of Buddhism, and the relativistic epistemology and ethics of Jainism.

3. **Orthodox Traditions (Āstika Systems)**
   Study the six classical Vedic-accepting systems (*Ṣaḍ-darśana*): the logic and ontology of Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, the dualistic cosmology and mind-discipline of Sāṃkhya-Yoga, and the hermeneutics and non-dual metaphysics of Mīmāṃsā-Vedānta.

4. **Shared Philosophical Frameworks and Cross-Cutting Debates**
   Synthesize your knowledge by engaging in the major inter-school debates across Indian epistemology (*pramāṇa-vāda* and theories of error), metaphysics (the self vs. no-self debate and models of causality), and moral philosophy (*dharma*, karma, and *mokṣa*).
