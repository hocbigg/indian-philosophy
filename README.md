## Introduction

The Indian Philosophy curriculum is a **complete education in Indian Philosophy** using online materials.

## Curriculum

### Nature and Scope of Indian Philosophy

This subject introduces the defining characteristics of Indian philosophy, exploring its practical orientation toward liberation, the classification of schools (*darśanas*), and the role of foundational texts (*śāstras*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - A comprehensive open-access textbook whose introductory chapter outlines the shared assumptions, common terminology, and general outlook of the Indian philosophical tradition.

[Indian Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction (Oxford University Press / Sue Hamilton)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780192853745) - A concise, conceptually oriented alternative to Chatterjee and Datta that introduces beginners to the core concerns of Indian thinkers without excessive scholastic terminology.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - A structured university video lecture series whose opening lectures provide an instructional overview of philosophical methods, terminology, and historical divisions.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - A complementary audio lecture series that contextualizes Indian thought within the broader global history of ideas, beginning with introductory reflections on the tradition's aims.


### Vedic and Upaniṣadic Foundations

This subject examines the foundational concepts of cosmic order (*ṛta*), ultimate reality (*brahman*), the inner self (*ātman*), and the historical emergence of the orthodox (*āstika*) and heterodox (*nāstika*) divide.

[The Essentials of Indian Philosophy (George Allen & Unwin / M. Hiriyanna)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.506393) - An open-access historical textbook whose opening chapters provide a lucid, foundational exposition of early Vedic thought and Upaniṣadic philosophy.

[Upanisads (Oxford World's Classics / Patrick Olivelle)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199540259) - The canonical English translation of the principal Upaniṣads; study this primary source alongside Hiriyanna to examine key dialogues concerning Brahman and Ātman firsthand.

[Hinduism Through Its Scriptures (HarvardX / Parimal Patil)](https://www.edx.org/learn/religion/harvard-university-hinduism-through-its-scriptures) - A free audit-mode course that provides complementary video lectures and guided textual readings on the Vedas, the Upaniṣads, and their enduring philosophical themes.


### Cārvāka: Materialism and Epistemic Empiricism

This subject explores the radical empiricism, metaphysical physicalism, skepticism regarding unperceived entities, and hedonistic ethics of the Cārvāka (Lokāyata) tradition.

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 2 provides the primary reading for this topic, detailing Cārvāka’s critique of inference, rejection of an immortal soul, and ethical hedonism.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 3 and 4 serve as a direct lecture companion to the reading, analyzing how Cārvāka challenged orthodox epistemology and forced rival schools to refine their proofs for inference.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episode 11 ("Mind the Gap: The Cārvākas") offers an accessible conversational overview examining why orthodox opponents took materialist challenges so seriously.


### Jaina Philosophy: Pluralistic Realism and Standpoint Epistemology

This subject covers the Jaina dualism between conscious souls (*jīva*) and non-conscious entities (*ajīva*), the doctrine of multi-sided reality (*anekāntavāda*), the logic of qualified assertions (*syādvāda*), and ethical non-harm (*ahiṃsā*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 3 provides the foundational textbook survey covering Jaina ontology, karmic bondage, and spiritual liberation.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 29 through 31 offer a structured academic exposition of Jaina epistemology, focusing on the sevenfold predication scheme (*saptabhaṅgī-naya*).

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 12 through 14 serve as a conceptual audio companion, breaking down how Jaina standpoint epistemology resolves seemingly conflicting philosophical assertions.


### Buddhist Philosophy: Dependent Origination, Non-Self, and Emptiness

This subject traces early Buddhist doctrines of suffering and causality, the reductionist analysis of personhood (*anattā* and the five *skandhas*), and the subsequent developments of Madhyamaka emptiness (*śūnyatā*) and Yogācāra consciousness-only metaphysics.

[Buddhism as Philosophy: An Introduction (Hackett Publishing / Mark Siderits)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781624669811) - The primary modern textbook for this subject, offering an analytically rigorous examination of Buddhist arguments from early Nikāya teachings to Mahāyāna schools.

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 4 serves as an open-access foundational alternative to Siderits, offering a systematic historical overview of the Four Noble Truths and early philosophical schools.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 15 through 24 provide an expansive, complementary audio lecture sequence detailing the transition from early Abhidharma metaphysics to Nāgārjuna’s dialectic and Vasubandhu’s idealism.


### Nyāya: Epistemology, Logic, and the Syllogism

This subject covers the Nyāya realistic epistemology, the validation of the four sources of knowledge (*pramāṇas*), the formal structure of the five-step inference (*anumāna*), and the taxonomy of fallacies (*hetvābhāsa*).

[The Nyaya Theory of Knowledge (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee)](https://archive.org/details/TheNyayaTheoryOfKnowledge) - A seminal, public-domain monograph that serves as the comprehensive core text for studying Nyāya theories of perception, inference, comparison, and testimony.

[The Character of Logic in India (SUNY Press / Bimal Krishna Matilal)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780791437407) - A concise, modern text that complements Chatterjee by analyzing the development of Indian debate rules, the nature of the inductive sign, and the formal structure of the Nyāya syllogism.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 15 through 20 deliver sequential classroom instruction illustrating the step-by-step operation of the five-membered Nyāya inferential argument.


### Vaiśeṣika: Categorical Ontology and Atomism

This subject explores the Vaiśeṣika ontology of the seven categories of reality (*padārthas*), atomic pluralism, and the causal doctrine of the new production of effects (*asatkāryavāda*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 6 acts as the core introductory reading, systematically categorizing substance, quality, action, generality, particularity, inherence, and non-existence.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 32 through 37 expand upon the reading with visual blackboard lectures detailing atomic combination and the mechanics of causation.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 29 through 31 provide an accessible companion discussion comparing Vaiśeṣika atomism and categorical realism with Western metaphysics.


### Sāṃkhya: Dualistic Metaphysics and Cosmic Evolution

This subject examines the metaphysical dualism between pure consciousness (*puruṣa*) and primordial matter (*prakṛti*), the three constitutive strands (*guṇas*), and the pre-existence of the effect within the cause (*satkāryavāda*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 7 presents the foundational textual exposition of Sāṃkhya cosmic evolution, the nature of the intellect (*buddhi*), and the arguments establishing the existence of Puruṣa.

[The Essentials of Indian Philosophy (George Allen & Unwin / M. Hiriyanna)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.506393) - Chapter 5 offers a concise, complementary historical overview that clarifies how Sāṃkhya dualism differs from Cartesian dualism and prepares the conceptual ground for classical Yoga.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 5 through 10 provide an in-depth video lecture series explaining the Sāṃkhya proof for *satkāryavāda* and the teleological unfolding of nature.


### Classical Yoga: Psychology and Mental Discipline

This subject addresses Patañjali’s system of psychological analysis, the cessation of mental fluctuations (*citta-vṛtti-nirodha*), the eight limbs of spiritual practice (*aṣṭāṅga-yoga*), and the attainment of absolute spiritual isolation (*kaivalya*).

[The Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali: A New Edition, Translation, and Commentary (North Point Press / Edwin F. Bryant)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780865477360) - The primary reference translation and guided commentary, offering exhaustive explanations drawing directly from classical commentators like Vyāsa and Vācaspati Miśra.

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 8 serves as a free open-access summary for learners seeking a concise, non-technical overview of the eight limbs and the cognitive stages of concentration (*samādhi*).

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 11 through 14 provide structured lectures explaining the relationship between Sāṃkhya metaphysics and Yoga's practical methods for calming mental modifications.


### Mīmāṃsā: Hermeneutics, Language, and Epistemic Validity

This subject examines Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā’s principles of textual interpretation, the eternal nature of the word-meaning relationship, the theory that cognitions are intrinsically valid (*svataḥ-prāmāṇyavāda*), and the nature of moral injunctions (*vidhi*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 9 provides the core reading on Mīmāṃsā epistemology, analyzing the sources of knowledge accepted by the Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara sub-schools.

[Indian Philosophy (NPTEL / IIT Madras / Dr. Satya Sundar Sethy)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109106059) - Lectures 38 through 40 provide detailed video lectures contrasting how Kumārila Bhaṭṭa and Prabhākara explain the emergence and invalidation of erroneous beliefs.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 40 and 41 supply an intuitive audio introduction explaining why Mīmāṃsā philosophers defended the default validity of human cognition and scriptural authority.


### Vedānta: Non-Dualism, Qualified Non-Dualism, and Dualism

This subject explores the major theological and philosophical interpretations of the Upaniṣads and *Brahma Sūtras*, focusing on Śaṅkara’s absolute non-dualism (Advaita), Rāmānuja’s qualified non-dualism (Viśiṣṭādvaita), and Madhva’s dualism (Dvaita).

[The Essentials of Indian Philosophy (George Allen & Unwin / M. Hiriyanna)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.506393) - Chapters 7 and 8 serve as the primary foundational texts, clearly contrasting Śaṅkara’s doctrine of illusion (*māyā*) with theistic and realist Vedāntic schools.

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (University of Calcutta / Satischandra Chatterjee and Dhirendramohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/IntroductionToIndianPhilosophy) - Chapter 10 functions as an alternative, comprehensive reference detailing the distinct levels of reality (*paramārtha* vs. *vyavahāra*) in Advaita Vedānta.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 45 through 54 provide a thorough audio lecture series tracing the debate over whether the individual soul is identical to, an attribute of, or eternally distinct from the Divine.


### Comparative Indian Epistemology: Pramāṇas and Theories of Error

This subject synthesizes knowledge across traditions by examining inter-school debates on what constitutes a valid instrument of knowing (*pramāṇa*), the definition of truth, and rival explanations of perceptual illusion (*khyātivāda*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (Cambridge University Press / Roy W. Perrett)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521618694) - Chapter 2 provides the core reading for this subject, organizing classical epistemological controversies into modern analytical themes such as justification, perception, and testimony.

[The Six Ways of Knowing: A Critical Study of the Advaita Theory of Knowledge (George Allen & Unwin / Dhirendra Mohan Datta)](https://archive.org/details/TheSixWaysOfKnowing1960D.M.Datta) - A definitive open-access comparative work; read this sequentially after Perrett for exhaustive analyses of perception, inference, testimony, comparison, postulation (*arthāpatti*), and non-apprehension (*anupalabdhi*).

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 28 and 30 serve as a lecture companion illustrating the high-stakes debates over whether knowledge can be acquired through testimony and how perceptual errors are recognized.


### Comparative Metaphysics: Self, Causality, and World

This subject investigates the central metaphysical disputes running through classical Indian thought, particularly the substantial self (*ātman*) versus no-self (*anattā*) debate and rival models of causation (*satkāryavāda*, *asatkāryavāda*, and *pratītyasamutpāda*).

[An Introduction to Indian Philosophy (Cambridge University Press / Roy W. Perrett)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521618694) - Chapter 3 serves as the primary modern text, comparing Buddhist reductionism, Nyāya substance ontology, and Vedāntic non-dualism regarding personal identity and universal causation.

[Outlines of Indian Philosophy (George Allen & Unwin / M. Hiriyanna)](https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.8889) - An open-access classic that provides a complementary historical treatment tracking how different causal doctrines directly shaped each school's view of liberation.

[Classical Indian Philosophy (History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps / Peter Adamson and Jonardon Ganeri)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/india) - Episodes 26 and 30 provide a lively audio discussion exploring the dialectical battle lines drawn between Buddhist nominalists and Brahmanical essentialists.


### Ethics, Action, and Liberation: Dharma, Karma, and Mokṣa

This subject evaluates classical Indian moral and spiritual values, focusing on the four human goals (*puruṣārthas*), the mechanics of moral consequence (*karma*), duties (*dharma*), and differing models of spiritual liberation (*mokṣa*, *nirvāṇa*, and *kaivalya*).

[Hindu Ethics: A Philosophical Study (University of Hawaii Press / Roy W. Perrett)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780824820855) - The core text for this subject, examining the philosophical coherence of karma, the duties of the social order (*svadharma*), and the ethical justification for ascetic renunciation.

[Outlines of Indian Philosophy (George Allen & Unwin / M. Hiriyanna)](https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.8889) - The concluding chapters serve as an open-access resource surveying the various ideals of life, comparing the living liberation (*jīvanmukti*) of Advaita with the isolation (*kaivalya*) of Yoga and the cessation of suffering (*nirvāṇa*) in Buddhism.

[Hinduism Through Its Scriptures (HarvardX / Parimal Patil)](https://www.edx.org/learn/religion/harvard-university-hinduism-through-its-scriptures) - The course modules on the *Bhagavad Gītā* offer a video-based companion that explores the synthesis of duty, knowledge, and selfless action (*niṣkāma karma*).
