# Advanced Topics

Learners who have mastered the foundational core are expected to select one or two specialization tracks aligned with their intellectual or research goals—not to complete every track sequentially.

- **[Advanced Epistemology, Logic, and Navya-Nyāya](#advanced-epistemology-logic-and-navya-nyāya):** Explores formal theories of inference, epistemic justification, radical nominalism, and the technical language of late classical scholastic logic.
- **[Philosophy of Language, Semantics, and Hermeneutics](#philosophy-of-language-semantics-and-hermeneutics):** Investigates sentence-meaning holism, compositionality, negative semantics (*apoha*), and aesthetic suggestion (*rasa-dhvani*).
- **[Advanced Metaphysics, Philosophy of Mind, and Non-Dualisms](#advanced-metaphysics-philosophy-of-mind-and-non-dualisms):** Analyzes Madhyamaka anti-foundationalism, Yogācāra phenomenology, late dialectical Advaita, and Kashmiri Pratyabhijñā monism.
- **[Moral, Legal, and Political Philosophy](#moral-legal-and-political-philosophy):** Examines epic moral dilemmas, classical political realism (*arthaśāstra*), modern anti-colonial thought, and caste critique.
- **[Cross-Cultural, Analytic, and Contemporary Engagements](#cross-cultural-analytic-and-contemporary-engagements):** Bridges classical Indian models of mind, knowledge, and rationality with modern cognitive science, analytic epistemology, and postcolonial metaphilosophy.

## Advanced Epistemology, Logic, and Navya-Nyāya

This track covers the formal development of classical Indian epistemology and logic, emphasizing late scholastic innovations, the Buddhist critique of realism, and technical theories of inference.

Pramāṇa Theory, Factivism, and Justification: [Epistemology in Classical India: The Knowledge Sources of the Nyāya School (Stephen Phillips / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138008816) - Analyzes Nyāya reliabilist and factivist models of knowledge, epistemic instrumenthood, and the classical debate between intrinsic and extrinsic justification.

The Buddhist Epistemological Revolution: [Recognizing Reality: Dharmakīrti's Philosophy and Its Tibetan Interpretations (Georges B. J. Dreyfus / SUNY Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780791430989) - Examines Dignāga and Dharmakīrti’s radical nominalist epistemology, the reduction of knowledge sources to perception and inference, and the causal criterion of reality.

Navya-Nyāya Formal Logic: [Materials for the Study of Navya-Nyāya Logic (Daniel H. H. Ingalls / Harvard University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9788120803848) - Introduces the formal technical syntax, relational abstractions, and pervasion theories (*vyāpti*) developed by Gaṅgeśa to eliminate linguistic ambiguity in scholastic debate.

Theories of Perceptual Error: [Perception: An Essay on Classical Indian Theories of Knowledge (Bimal Krishna Matilal / Oxford Clarendon Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198246251) - Provides a foundational analytical study of classical theories of illusion (*khyātivāda*), direct realism, and the intentionality of misperception.


## Philosophy of Language, Semantics, and Hermeneutics

This track investigates Sanskrit philosophical analyses of meaning, word-world relations, compositional semantics, negative reference, and aesthetic suggestion.

The Sphoṭa Doctrine and Linguistic Monism: [The Word and the World: India's Contribution to the Study of Language (Bimal Krishna Matilal / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195625158) - Explores Bhartṛhari's linguistic metaphysics (*Śabda-brahman*), sentence-meaning holism, and the philosophical inseparability of thought and word.

Mīmāṃsā Hermeneutics and Compositionality: [Indian Theories of Meaning (K. Kunjunni Raja / Adyar Library and Research Centre)](https://archive.org/details/indiantheoriesof0000kunj) - A comprehensive open-access study comparing the Prabhākara and Bhāṭṭa debates on whether words convey independent referents prior to syntactical connection.

Buddhist Nominalism and Negative Semantics: [Apoha: Buddhist Nominalism and Human Cognition (Mark Siderits, Tom Tillemans, and Arindam Chakrabarti, eds. / Columbia University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780231153614) - Investigates how Buddhist logicians construct conceptual categories and general terms entirely through differential exclusion (*anyāpoha*) without positing real universals.

Sanskrit Poetics and Aesthetic Suggestion: [The Dhvanyāloka with the Locana (Ānandavardhana and Abhinavagupta, trans. Daniel H. H. Ingalls, J. M. Masson, and M. V. Patwardhan / Harvard University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674207806) - Translates and comments on the foundational texts of aesthetic theory, showing how poetic suggestion (*dhvani*) produces aesthetic resonance (*rasa*) beyond literal semantics.


## Advanced Metaphysics, Philosophy of Mind, and Non-Dualisms

This track explores radical metaphysical anti-essentialism, Buddhist phenomenological psychology, dialectical non-dualism, and dynamic monism.

Madhyamaka Dialectic and Anti-Essentialism: [Nāgārjuna's Metaphysics: A Philosophical Investigation (Jan Westerhoff / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195384963) - Systematically reconstructs Nāgārjuna's critique of intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*), the Two Truths doctrine, and ontological anti-foundationalism.

Yogācāra Phenomenology and Reflexive Awareness: [Perceiving Reality: Consciousness, Intentionality, and Cognition in Buddhist Philosophy (Christian Coseru / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780190253110) - Connects Yogācāra models of mind-only (*vijñaptimātra*), the storehouse consciousness (*ālayavijñāna*), and reflexive awareness (*svasaṃvedana*) with contemporary phenomenology.

Dialectical Advaita and the Critique of Realism: [Philosophy and Argument in Late Vedānta: Śrī Harṣa's Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya (Phyllis Granoff / Reidel)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9789400998247) - Analyzes Śrīharṣa's negative dialectic, which systematically demolishes Nyāya realist definitions of knowledge and difference to establish non-dualism.

Pratyabhijñā Dynamic Monism: [Rediscovering God with Transcendental Argument: A Contemporary Interpretation of Monistic Kashmiri Śaiva Philosophy (David Peter Lawrence / SUNY Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780791440582) - Explores Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta’s synthesis of non-dual consciousness (*prakāśa*) with dynamic, self-reflective power (*vimarśa*).


## Moral, Legal, and Political Philosophy

This track examines moral conflicts in narrative literature, classical treatises on political realism and jurisprudence, modern anti-colonial frameworks, and radical caste critiques.

Epic Dilemmas and Practical Ethics: [Ethics and Epics: The Collected Essays of Bimal Krishna Matilal, Vol. II (Bimal Krishna Matilal / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195655117) - Evaluates moral conflict (*dharma-saṃkaṭa*), moral luck, and ethical ambiguity through critical readings of the *Mahābhārata*.

Political Realism and Governance: [King, Governance, and Law in Ancient India: Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra (Kauṭilya, trans. Patrick Olivelle / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199891825) - An authoritative translation and analysis of classical Indian statecraft, political pragmatism, espionage systems, and interstate relations (*maṇḍala* theory).

Modern Indian Political Thought: [Introduction to Modern Indian Political Thought (NPTEL / IIT Guwahati / Prof. Mithilesh Kumar Jha)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109103135) - A 30-lecture university course examining how thinkers from Rammohan Roy to Gandhi, Tagore, and Lohia re-articulated autonomy, nationalism, and *swaraj*.

Caste Critique and Social Justice: [Annihilation of Caste: The Annotated Critical Edition (B. R. Ambedkar, ed. S. Anand / Verso)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781781688311) - A seminal twentieth-century critique deconstructing the metaphysical and scriptural rationalizations for caste hierarchy while arguing for constitutional democracy and fraternity.

Caste Critique and Social Justice: [The Annihilation of Caste (Dr. B. R. Ambedkar / Columbia Center for Teaching and Learning)](https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/index.html) - A freely accessible online study edition of Ambedkar's core address, accompanied by textual annotations and historical documentation.


## Cross-Cultural, Analytic, and Contemporary Engagements

This track places classical Indian philosophical insights into dialogue with modern analytic epistemology, cognitive neuroscience, philosophy of mind, and decolonial historiography.

Self, Consciousness, and Cognitive Science: [Waking, Dreaming, Being: Self and Consciousness in Neuroscience, Meditation, and Philosophy (Evan Thompson / Columbia University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780231137096) - Integrates classical Indian debates on consciousness and the self (Nyāya, Advaita, and Buddhism) with cognitive neuroscience and phenomenological philosophy of mind.

Self, Consciousness, and Cognitive Science: [Contemporary Issues in Philosophy of Mind & Cognition (NPTEL / IIT Bombay / Prof. Ranjan K. Panda and Prof. Rajakishore Nath)](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/109101004) - A 42-lecture university video course bridging Indian models of consciousness from the Upaniṣads with modern functionalism, intentionality, and artificial intelligence.

Comparative Analytic Epistemology: [Realisms Interlinked: Objects, Subjects and Other Subjects (Arindam Chakrabarti / Bloomsbury Academic)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781350085350) - Brings classical Indian realism into direct conversation with contemporary Western problems of perception, other minds, and indexicality.

Rationality and Decolonial Historiography: [Philosophy in Classical India: The Proper Work of Reason (Jonardon Ganeri / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780415264341) - Reconstructs classical Indian philosophy as an argumentative culture of rational inquiry, dismantling colonial assumptions of Indian thought as purely mystical.

Contemporary Critique of Traditional Categories: [Indian Philosophy: A Counter Perspective (Daya Krishna / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195626629) - A modern critical work that deconstructs standard textbook narratives, demonstrating that classical Indian thought was dynamic, pluralistic, and never tethered solely to spiritual liberation.
