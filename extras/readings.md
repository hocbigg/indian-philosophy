# Great Readings

A curated collection of field-defining primary texts, groundbreaking monographs, and seminal philosophical investigations in Indian philosophy. These works demonstrate how classical and modern thinkers formulated paradigms, constructed formal logical systems, and challenged dogmatic orthodoxy.

## Foundational Primary Classics and Landmark Commentaries

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *The Principal Upaniṣads* | S. Radhakrishnan (Ed. & Trans.) | Primary Text Edition | The foundational textual sourcebook containing the thirteen major Vedic-Upaniṣadic dialogues on the nature of Brahman, Ātman, and ultimate reality. |
| *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way)* | Nāgārjuna (Trans. Jay L. Garfield) | Primary Text & Commentary | The foundational treatise of Madhyamaka Buddhism, presenting an uncompromising dialectical deconstruction of intrinsic nature (svabhāva) through dependent origination. |
| *The Nyāya-Sūtras of Gautama with the Commentary of Vātsyāyana* | Gautama & Vātsyāyana (Trans. Ganganatha Jha) | Classical Commentary | The seminal treatise establishing Indian realism, five-stage inferential syllogisms, epistemology (pramāṇa), and formal rules of disputation (vāda). |
| *The Brahma Sūtra Śaṅkara Bhāṣya* | Śaṅkarācārya (Trans. Swami Gambhirananda) | Classical Commentary | The crowning masterpiece of Advaita Vedānta, constructing a rigorous non-dualist metaphysics that reconciles Upaniṣadic revelation with rational exegesis. |

## Epistemology, Logic, and Philosophy of Language

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Perception: An Essay on Classical Indian Theories of Knowledge* | Bimal Krishna Matilal | Monograph | A landmark philosophical study comparing classical Indian debates on perception, direct realism, and phenomenalism with contemporary analytic epistemology. |
| *The Character of Logic in India* | Bimal Krishna Matilal (Eds. Jonardon Ganeri & Heeraman Tiwari) | Monograph | The definitive reassessment of Indian logic, demonstrating how Nyāya and Buddhist inference operate as epistemically grounded logics of rational support rather than formal tautologies. |
| *Recognizing Reality: Dharmakīrti's Philosophy and Its Tibetan Interpretations* | Georges B. J. Dreyfus | Monograph | A comprehensive, rigorous investigation of Dharmakīrti's epistemological turn, nominalism, perception, and causal efficacy (arthakriyā). |
| *The Word and the World: India's Contribution to the Study of Language* | Bimal Krishna Matilal | Monograph | The essential exploration of Sanskrit linguistic philosophy, analyzing Bhartṛhari’s sentence-holism (sphoṭa), Mīmāṃsā compositionality, and Buddhist negative semantics (apoha). |

## Metaphysics, Consciousness, and Non-Dualisms

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Nāgārjuna's Metaphysics: A Philosophical Investigation* | Jan Westerhoff | Monograph | An analytical reconstruction of Nāgārjuna's anti-foundationalism, systematically examining emptiness across physical objects, causality, motion, and the self. |
| *The Concealed Art of the Soul: Theories of Self and Practices of Truth in Indian Ethics and Epistemology* | Jonardon Ganeri | Monograph | A transformative study exploring first-person subjectivity, introspection, and the classical debates between substantialist ātman and Buddhist anātman. |
| *Buddhist Phenomenology: A Philosophical Investigation of Yogācāra Buddhism* | Dan Lusthaus | Monograph | A groundbreaking reassessment arguing that Yogācāra is not metaphysical idealism, but an advanced phenomenological critique of cognitive reification and intentionality. |
| *The Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā of Utpaladeva with the Author's Vṛtti* | Utpaladeva (Trans. Raffaele Torella) | Primary Text & Critical Edition | The foundational text of the Kashmir Pratyabhijñā school, articulating a dynamic non-dualism where consciousness is both self-luminous (prakāśa) and self-reflective (vimarśa). |

## Ethics, Epic Hermeneutics, and Political Philosophy

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Ethics and Epics: The Collected Essays of Bimal Krishna Matilal, Vol. II* | Bimal Krishna Matilal (Ed. Jonardon Ganeri) | Essay Collection | A profound analysis of moral dilemmas (dharma-saṃkaṭa), moral luck, and the ethical architecture of the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa. |
| *King, Governance, and Law in Ancient India: Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra* | Kauṭilya (Trans. Patrick Olivelle) | Primary Text & Critical Edition | The definitive critical translation of ancient India's foundational work on political realism, statecraft, intelligence networks, and jurisprudence. |
| *Annihilation of Caste* | B. R. Ambedkar | Landmark Political Treatise | A blistering, philosophically rigorous critique of caste hierarchy, Brahmanical orthodoxy, and the normative authority of the Dharmaśāstras. |
| *Hind Swaraj (Indian Home Rule)* | M. K. Gandhi | Political Philosophical Monograph | A radical philosophical critique of modern industrial civilization, conceptualizing autonomy (swaraj) as ethical self-mastery and non-violent resistance (ahiṃsā). |

## Comparative, Analytic, and Decolonial Reconstructions

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *The Self: Naturalism, Consciousness, and the First-Person Stance* | Jonardon Ganeri | Monograph | An ambitious cross-cultural masterpiece constructing an embodied, naturalist theory of selfhood using classical Indian systems and contemporary philosophy of mind. |
| *Waking, Dreaming, Being: Self and Consciousness in Neuroscience, Meditation, and Philosophy* | Evan Thompson | Monograph | A pioneering synthesis integrating Indian Buddhist and Advaita Vedānta theories of consciousness with contemporary cognitive neuroscience and phenomenology. |
| *Indian Philosophy: A Counter Perspective* | Daya Krishna | Monograph | A provocative, iconoclastic work that dismantles pervasive dogmas surrounding Indian philosophy, rejecting the myth that it is purely spiritual, theological, or non-discursive. |
| *Philosophy in Classical India: The Proper Work of Reason* | Jonardon Ganeri | Monograph | A field-defining study demonstrating that classical Indian thought is fundamentally characterized by rigorous, autonomous rational inquiry and debate. |
